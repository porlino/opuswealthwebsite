Tax & retirement planning

How high earners can coordinate tax strategy with retirement contributions

Opus Wealth Research · Educational brief

Retirement contributions should not be treated as an isolated annual tax decision. The better question is how today’s deduction, future taxation, account flexibility, and expected spending work together across decades.

A practical sequence

  1. Capture the full employer match.
  2. Maintain adequate liquidity before maximizing locked accounts.
  3. Compare traditional and Roth contributions using current and expected future marginal rates.
  4. Coordinate workplace plans, IRAs, taxable investing, and charitable goals.

What deserves annual review

  • Income and contribution limits
  • Tax-bracket changes and phaseouts
  • Backdoor Roth documentation
  • Beneficiary designations
  • Withdrawal flexibility before and after retirement

For 2026, the IRS increased several retirement-plan contribution limits. Because limits and individual eligibility change, decisions should be verified for the applicable tax year.

Macro perspective

Inflation resilience: why nominal growth is not enough

A portfolio can rise in dollar value while losing purchasing power. The relevant scorecard is the return remaining after inflation, taxes, fees, and withdrawals—not the headline return alone.

Resilience comes from matching assets to time horizons: near-term spending needs liquidity; intermediate obligations require stability; long-term capital can carry more growth risk.

Questions for a resilient plan

  • How many years of planned spending are protected from market volatility?
  • Which assets have historically responded differently to inflation surprises?
  • Are taxes forcing sales at unfavorable times?
  • Is portfolio income actually keeping pace with rising costs?

Business owners

Designing an exit plan before a buyer appears

The strongest exit plans begin while the owner still has choices. Waiting for an offer can compress the timeline for valuation work, tax planning, operational cleanup, and family decisions.

Build readiness early

  • Separate personal and business financial planning.
  • Reduce dependence on the owner.
  • Strengthen recurring revenue and documentation.
  • Understand likely deal structures and tax consequences.

Plan the life after liquidity

A sale price is only one variable. Owners should model net proceeds, reinvestment risk, future cash flow, charitable intent, family transfers, and what meaningful work looks like after closing.

Attorneys, tax professionals, valuation specialists, and the wealth advisor should coordinate well before negotiations become binding.

Family wealth strategy

Legacy structures that preserve both family harmony and capital

Estate documents direct assets, but they do not create shared understanding. Durable planning aligns legal structure, account ownership, beneficiary designations, insurance, family communication, and the capabilities of future decision-makers.

The essential alignment check

  • Wills and trusts reflect current intent.
  • Beneficiary designations match the plan.
  • Successor decision-makers understand their roles.
  • Heirs receive age-appropriate financial education.

The goal is not control for its own sake. It is to create enough structure to protect people without preventing them from developing judgment. A family meeting, written statement of intent, and clear professional contact list can be as important as the documents themselves.

Estate and tax laws change. Legal structures should be prepared and reviewed by qualified counsel.
